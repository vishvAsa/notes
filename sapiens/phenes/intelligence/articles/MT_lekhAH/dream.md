It is true that at least some dreams, especially ones that feature a repeating motif, are symbolic. The motif symbolizes something that is not directly revealed. Why this should happen has been a great mystery to me. 

If it were to convey a message for self-improvement, then it would be better to present it directly. 

If it were to prevent fright, then it fails, as these dream motifs often conclude with a frightening element that wakes you up. 

Often, you cannot interpret it yourself -- you notice the recurrent motif, but you do not know what it means and why it recurs. 

Thus, its evolutionary basis remains unclear, though biological necessity requires all animals to sleep and possibly dream. 

If one goes to an interpreter, then one is at the mercy of their whims. Thus, the meaning of a dream often remains a mystery to the dreamer.

> Within Hindu metaphysics, the explanation seems more straight forward, I think. During sleep you are closer to Consciousness. As we ascend towards Consciousness, thinking is more symbolic and less linear. dreams are symbolic as they are flashes of insight from a higher plane

As a metaphysical explanation I too find that satisfying. However, what I'm unclear about is how it squares with the biology.

> I’m feeling that the neurobiological understanding is  limited/misleading. Eg: comatose and sleeping patient both have same level of consciousness to external observer. Yet emerging from deep sleep one feels refreshed and active, but emerging from coma one is groggy and slow.

As for sleep you statement is exactly in line with what Adi sha~NkarAcharya said.
Yet, given your background I find it interesting that you say that the neurobiological paradigm is insufficient. I wonder if you reached that point after some exploration or you always felt so.

> It was sometime during my residency that I gradually began to notice that Hindu explanations on consciousness/sleep/neuro stuff were actually making far more sense than what modern neuroscience offers. It was a switch, not at all something I began with. Not a critique of modern neuroscience of course. It is powerful and verifiably useful in its place. And it is my livelihood.

I get your position. I know such positions get a lot of criticism from many within science and the scientistic lay populace.  
However, I agree that there is much that remains un-understood at the consciousness-neurobiology interface. I think there will be a consistent biological theory for which have many of big pieces in play.  
However, I think the realm of consciousness, the "Platonic realm" and the like are real (as ancient H discovered) and will intersect with the biology in ways we are far from knowing. Then more recently we have had the AIs which so far strengthen a biology only position but I have not given up on the other above-mentioned realms.
