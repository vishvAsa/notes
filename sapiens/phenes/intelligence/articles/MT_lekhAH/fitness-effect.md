## Fixation
Saw a debate between two u1 intellectuals on this medium. One of them held the rather peculiar position that genetic variants favoring higher intelligence will go to fixation in every population, and insinuated that there might not be any major trade-offs for increasing intelligence. 

First, we empirically observe that in humans, high-end intelligence may come with compromises in other dimensions: as we have remarked, the line between genius and madness can be a thin one!  
Thus, there does seem to be trade-offs for intelligence, something the Han boosterist Hsu also seems to downplay/deny on the hereditarian side.

Second, studies across a wide range of species suggest that the relative size of the brain (encephalization) does seem to correlate with intelligence. Thus, one can use brain size as a proxy for intelligence. Clearly, there are trade-offs for what we can carry in our heads. 

That is the reason we are not going around with a data center in the upper story. As the head increases in size, it makes mammalian live birth harder.  
It is energetically more demanding. It probably needs a longer "childhood" during which the neural network can be trained.  
This, in turn, cuts into successful reproductive maturity.+++(5)+++  
It also probably cuts into the window for self-sufficient survival, thereby reducing fitness. 

One can approximately represent this numerically, thus:  
Let $y$ be fitness and $x$ the brain-size metric. Then, in a simplest scenario, fitness will increase as some positive power of $x$, i.e., $k_1 x^a$.  
However as $x$ rises, a cost also sets in, resulting in a cost term, again, a power of $x$, but with a negative constant: $-k_2 x^b$, where $b>a$    Thus, $y= k_1 x^a -k_2 x^b$ 

Here, the exponents $a, b$ will be determined by the selective landscape – broadly, the environment. Thus, in different parts of the world with different environments, they will assume different values.  
This curve takes the form shown below and will have a maximum depending on $a,b$. Thus, there will be an optimal intelligence as this curve will have only one maximum.  

Hence, from theory, one would expect that intelligence in a population would be centered on a specific mean value representing this optimum determined by the trade-offs the population faces.  
As these can vary between different environments, one would expect intelligence to diverge between groups.

## Success
Source: [TW](https://x.com/blog_supplement/status/2092086539647844805)

There is no doubt that IQ is a major factor for success in life. However, it is not the only one. Most people have probably seen some people with lower IQ than themselves do better than they do, and similarly, they do better than some with higher IQ than them. So, in addition to plain IQ, there are additional important factors. One could be the angle between the spatial/quantitative and verbal IQ vectors. We think, more importantly, it is some aspect of personality. However, this aspect of personality is hard to capture via standard personality tests. It relates to what we have called "national character" before.


## Memetic susceptibility
People in that above-average IQ range often, in certain real-life contexts, fare worse than those in the lower range as they tend to fool themselves more effectively into erroneous/patently contra-factual beliefs than the latter. The long-term consequences of this on the "smart fraction" is underappreciated relative to the more obvious correlation seen between them and measures of economic productivity like GDP or journal articles. Thus, it might be a dynamic where at some point the smart fraction catches a fatal or in the least destructive memetic infection.
