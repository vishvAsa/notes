## Angela
### Background
Ravaged by 2 men and their dog at age 16 (1988), Angela, an Italian American, developed mental instability and was institutionalized.  
Not visited by family, she would call religious places nearby.  
She was visited by church folk who twice impregnated her. Kids were sent to foster care system.  

### First contact
At age 34, in 2006, she called a university town ISKCON temple. Carl was sent. The institution was 1.5 hours away. After 2-3 visits, she offered her body; but was releived when told that it was not needed. Within some 5 months, she was ok. 

After 3 such months (month 8 of the visit) of normalcy, Carl suggested that she be taken off drugs. The female Doctor acted as if Carl was the enemy. 

> I would take her for ice cream, or sit by a lake and chat.

### Regression
Soon she was transferred to another facility 3 hours away from Carl, without any notice. There, she was back to her pre-contact disturbed state. The same doctor, when asked, just said "we're trying new drugs". 

> > So, at the end of yr 1 of contact she was transferred to the facility 3 hours away, where she was put back to square 1?

> Yes. "New drugs". No reason given in why it was considered necessary. After I started asking too many questions, I started to be kept out.

## Loss of contact
Despite repeated exclusion, Carl kept up sproadic phone contact 2007-2012. 

> > Did she show any sign of retaining her improved self?  
> 
> Hard to tell when you're not face to face but nowhere near the calm, cheerful and warm self she had become at 8 months. I think at some level she felt I had also abandoned her, so she was edgy and nervous.


> Angela is allowed calls - but who's going to take her seriously? Every single inmate wants to get out. It's like a jail. If they behave "good", they're allowed an hour outside with someone (like me) once a week. 

> Upto a point I was able to call her and vice versa. Then at one point they said she's been moved again and I couldn't even get a number. Don't know if that was true or not but eventually didn't follow up. Never heard from her either.

### Drug trials
प्रयोगार्थं जनसमूहं नियन्त्रयति सा महासंकायानां निमित्तम्। Her career was about drug trials, not on curing this group of patients.

> In the first hospital I got to know one of the staff - a  young girl. She seemed nice but only strictly followed instructions. The doctor - she looked like a witch - not exaggerating. Really weird and artificial in the way she talked to me.


> She is forced to take the drugs? Cant pretend otherwise?
>
> > No way. Total monitoring. It's like being a labrat. Literally. At the new facility I would see them lined up and made to sit like children on the floor in the corridors, awaiting further instructions. Stick and carrot policies we don't even use on children nowadays.

### No release

No one outside the system had the authority to get her released. 

> मम अधिकारः नासीत्।  तस्याः उपचार-गणः, मनोवैज्ञानिक-समीक्षा-गोष्ठी अथवा कोऽपि न्यायाधीश निर्णयं करोति चेत् सा बहिरागच्छेत्। परिवारस्य अपि अधिकारः नास्ति ! - ते केवलं न्यायालये याचितुम् अर्हन्ति।
यदा कोऽपि मनोविज्ञानचिकित्सातन्त्रे committed भवति तदारभ्य सः तेषामेव नखालौ जीवेत्। महानगरेषु सुशिक्षितपरिवारैः सह व्यवहारः भिन्नः, उपान्तेषु अल्पशिक्षितैः ग्रामीणैः सह भिन्नः।

## Ohio addiction center
This wasn't my only encounter with the mental health system in rural America. I once also did some IT work for addiction centers which are part of the same department. That was another strange experience. I didn't ask any questions but the types and scale of weirdness I saw were jarring. It made me think a lot about the link between psychology (and psychiatry) and religion/spirituality.
