Nope. "This is one of the strongest recent empirical demonstrations that evolutionary change in humans can and does occur on historical timescales.The Akbari et al. (2026) Nature paper shows that natural selection in West Eurasians has been pervasive and has actually accelerated over the last ~10,000 years—directly contradicting the idea that a thousand years (or even several millennia) is “literally nothing” for human evolution. 

The paper is “Ancient DNA reveals pervasive directional selection across West Eurasia” by Ali Akbari, Annabel Perry, Alison R. Barton, and colleagues (including senior author David Reich), published in Nature (volume 654, pages 419–428) on 15 April 2026 (DOI: 10.1038/s41586-026-10358-1).

findings relevant to rapid change Researchers analyzed 15,836 West Eurasian genomes (10,016 newly reported ancient ones + previously published ancient + modern samples) spanning roughly the last 18,000 years, with a focus on the last 10,000. They developed new statistical methods to detect consistent directional trends in allele-frequency change over time while controlling for migration, population structure, admixture, and drift. They identified 479 independent alleles (gene variants) with strong evidence of directional selection (positive or negative) in the past ~10 millennia. Earlier ancient-DNA studies had found only about 21 such clear cases. More than 60% of these variants have known associations with present-day traits (skin pigmentation/light skin and red hair, blood types, immune function, disease risks such as celiac/Crohn’s, lower risk of male-pattern baldness or alcoholism, etc.)

Selection accelerated after the transition to farming (and more intensely in the last ~5,000 years than in the preceding 5,000). Directional selection accounts for only ~2% of observed allele-frequency changes overall, but that still produces hundreds of clear signals because the dataset is so large. 

Median selection coefficients (|s|) at the tagged non-HLA loci are on the order of ~0.86% (range roughly 0.43–4.3% in reported summaries). Even modest coefficients compound over generations; strong or sustained selection can produce large frequency shifts in a few thousand years (or less). Examples include alleles rising from near 0% to substantial frequencies in 4,000–6,000 years. 

They also detected polygenic (multi-gene) shifts of roughly one standard deviation (on the scale of modern variation) in combinations of alleles that today predict traits such as lower body fat, reduced schizophrenia risk, and higher scores related to cognitive/performance measures. (The authors stress that modern trait associations do not automatically equal the ancient selective pressures, due to pleiotropy, environmental differences, and the fact that some modern phenotypes did not exist in the past.)

Why this matters for the “a thousand years is nothing” claim  
Human generation time is ~25–30 years, so 1,000 years ≈ 33–40 generations and 10,000 years ≈ 330–400 generations. Classic “hard sweeps” to fixation were rare over deeper human history, but the Akbari data show that soft/polygenic directional selection on standing variation has been widespread and strong enough in the Holocene (especially post-farming) to move hundreds of alleles substantially. Selection intensity increased with major lifestyle/environmental shifts (agriculture, denser settlements, new pathogens, diets, etc.). This is consistent with other lines of evidence that human evolution has continued (and in some respects sped up) under cultural and ecological short: far from being static for 100,000 years, West Eurasian genomes show clear, measurable, and accelerating natural selection over the last 10 millennia. A thousand years is enough time for detectable shifts underchange.In realistic selection coefficients, and multiple thousands of years have produced hundreds of them. The paper’s data and methods are publicly available (including an interactive browser), so the results can be examined directly.
