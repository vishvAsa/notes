## Cyril
The political influence of Coptic Christians has declined significantly.
Coptic bishops like Cyril of Alexandria once commanded their own private paramilitaries (parabalani) which would intimidate opponents, riot in the streets, etc. in order to enforce the bishop's will.
Their reputation for violence was so notorious that the Roman Emperors in Constantinople tried to curb their power by reducing their numbers, stripping the bishop of direct control over them, and placing them under the authority of the secular governor.  

Coptic Christianity became theologically distinct from other forms of Christianity following the Council of Chalcedon, but Coptic Christianity already had a unique cultural and spiritual identity by the late 3rd and early 4th century (e.g. Cenobitic monasticism of Pachomius).

Cyril of Alexandria was obviously more Hellenistic than Shenoute of Atripe, but he was still ethnically Egyptian and was likely fluent in Coptic.  
In any case, my point is that they went from being persecutors to being persecuted themselves.


## Shenoute
Shenoute was hardly better than Cyril.
Shenoute and Cyril were both close allies and mutual collaborators.
Shenoute was once accused of trespassing, theft, and looting after he led a monastic raid on the private estate of a pagan magistrate (likely Gesios).
The raiding Christian monks searched for and destroyed any cult statues and idols they could get their hand on.
When Shenoute was challenged by secular authorities for this violation of the law, his reply was essentially that divine law overrides imperial law.
One of his quotes "For just as there is no crime/robbery for those who have Jesus..." has become quite famous.

> "There is no crime for those who have Christ" - Xtian monk Shenoute's response to the accusation of attacking a pagans house in Roman Egypt.
