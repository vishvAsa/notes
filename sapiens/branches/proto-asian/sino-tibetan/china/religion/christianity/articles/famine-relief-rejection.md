Disaster victims' resistance to foreign relief was equally fervent.

In the third year of Guangxu (1877), Shandong's Chang County victims "refused foreign relief"; in Le'an County, victims "all preferred to starve to death rather than accept foreign relief." Henan victims were the most resolute, not only rejecting aid but vigorously propagating the missionaries' "ulterior motives." A Westerner once published in the *Shenbao*: "Henan's famine victims are utterly obtuse; they refuse to take the silver distributed by the missionaries, believing Western clerics intend to buy submission and lure converts, hence offering us silver-beware falling into their trap. They warn one another, and not a soul would accept.

Moreover, mimicking the style of *Illustrations of Iron Tears*, they fabricate all manner of atrocities, claiming missionaries lure people into the faith only to abuse them thus. They post these widely to stir up sentiment; provincial officials are powerless."

Despite this, missionaries repeatedly tried dispatching personnel to Henan for relief, but neither the populace nor the government permitted their stay, even warning that lingering would endanger their lives. Anti-missionary sentiment in Henan far exceeded other provinces; upon hearing of missionaries like Guo Xiang's arrival in Kaifeng, residents plastered the city with proclamations bearing lines like "Better eat barbarian flesh than barbarian grain," while a certain academy suspended classes, declaring "war on the Westerners."
