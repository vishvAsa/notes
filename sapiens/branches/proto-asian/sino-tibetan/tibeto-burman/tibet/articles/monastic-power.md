The way the clergy organised themselves in Tibet doesn't help at all lol, even before the Gelugpa the degree of concentrated in hands of clergy is kind of fascinating, much of the late medieval involved clans mediating power primarily through initiated monks of their lineages. There's a nice monograph on the Phagmodrugpa - "Medieval Rule in Tibet: The Rlangs Clan and the Political and Religious History of the Ruling House of Phag mo gru pa (2 Volumes)" that really enumerates this.

The most powerful state in post-Mongol Tibet literally emerged from the lay admins of the Kagyu monastery of Densatil and Phagmodru lol  
Clans would regularly wield power primarily as adminstrators and lay-attaches to clerical land and send some sons and daughters into the monastery to establish influences.
