When we think of the billionaires who supply firepower to global militaries, we picture elite tycoons born into old money, brokering deals in air-conditioned European boardrooms. We never picture a broke, 20 something man huddled for warmth on a freezing concrete platform at the Nagpur railway station, wondering if he will survive the night.

Born in a small village in Bhilwara, Rajasthan, Satyanarayan Nandlal Nuwal was not a prodigy; he was a serial failure. He dropped out of school after class 10 & tried his hand at business. By the time he was in his 20s, he had launched 3 different ventures: an ink manufacturing unit, a transport business & a small trading shop. All 3 crashed & burned, leaving his pockets completely empty & his family’s debt piling up.

In 1977, with nothing left to lose, he boarded a train to Nagpur. He was so incredibly broke that he could not afford a single night in a cheap lodge. For months, the Nagpur railway station became his home. He would wash his face in the public bathrooms, sleep on empty gunny bags on the platform & walk miles every day under the scorching sun just to save a few paise on bus fare.

His life changed when he met an explosives dealer who wanted to lease out his license. Nuwal knew absolutely nothing about chemicals/explosives, but he had a desperate hunger to survive. He leased the license, rented a tiny, hot warehouse for ₹1000 a month & started trading commercial explosives to coal mines.

But Nuwal was not content just shifting boxes for a commission. He noticed a massive, dangerous flaw in India's industrial backbone: the country's mining, infrastructure & defense systems were heavily dependent on foreign cartels/sluggish state run factories for basic explosives. If a global supply chain broke, India’s progress would stop dead in its tracks.

In 1995, he stopped trading & decided to manufacture. He founded Solar Industries.

Operating from a single, small manufacturing unit, Nuwal obsessed over the chemistry. He engineered industrial explosives that were safer, more stable & significantly cheaper than what foreign giants were dumping into the Indian market. His tech was so revolutionary that it did not just disrupt the mining sector; it caught the eye of the Indian Armed Forces.

Until then, private Indian companies were strictly banned from manufacturing military ammunition, it was an exclusive govt monopoly. But Nuwal’s track record for flawless quality & unmatched safety parameters broke the glass ceiling.

In 2010, Solar Industries became the 1st private company in Indian history to get a license to manufacture propellants & explosives for the Indian military.

Nuwal did not just make bullets. His factories began manufacturing the literal brains & muscles of India's defense architecture: warheads for the state of the art BrahMos & Pinaka missiles, advanced propellant boosters for space rockets & high tech multi mode hand grenades that replaced decades old vintage military gear. He turned a tiny trading shack into a multi-billion dollar strategic asset exporting defense tech to 50+ countries.

He spent his youth w/o a roof over his head, ignored by the 1000s of commuters rushing past his spot on a crowded railway platform, but Satyanarayan Nuwal grew up to build the literal firepower that protects the very skies of the nation that once left him out in the cold.
