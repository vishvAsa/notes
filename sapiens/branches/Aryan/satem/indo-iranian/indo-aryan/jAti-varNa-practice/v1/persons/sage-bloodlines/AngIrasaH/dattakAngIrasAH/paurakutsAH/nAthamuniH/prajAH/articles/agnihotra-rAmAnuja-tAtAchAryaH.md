Homage to an august centenarian


**V SUNDARAM |** Thu, 07 Aug, 2008 , 03:48 PM


**All the devotees and disciples of MAHAVIDWAN SHRI. U. VE. AGNIHOTRAM RAMANUJA THATHACHARIAR in different parts of India and the world are celebrating his birth centenary of this internationally known great vedic scholar today.**



**Mahavidwan Shri u ve agnihotram Ramanuja Thathachariar**

**Yogakshema Trust, Chennai, has organised the 11th Vedic Sammelan at Jain Kalyana Mandapam, Singrachari Street, Triplicane on the 10 of August 2008. At that Sammelan, prominent scholars like N.S.Ramanuja Thathachraya, Former Vice Chancellor of Rashtriya Kendriya Samskrit Vidya Peet, Tirupathi, Dr. R Krishnamurthy, former Principal of Sanskrit College, Madras, Dr. V Kannan, Pro-Vice Chancellor, Central University, Hyderabad and Dr. O R Devanathan, lecturer, Presidency College and many other scholars would be offering their felicitations to Agnihotram Swamy on his completion of 100 years of dedicated and unique service to the cause of our Vedic Heritage. In every sense of the word, Aghihothram Swami’s many-splendoured life has been a life of unmatched creative fulfillment – a life of selfless love and service, guided by his Himalayan knowledge and wisdom.**

The Vedas lay down that every individual should discharge three pious obligations. The three pious obligations were:  
(1) Towards God (**_Devaruna),_**  
(2) Towards Parents **_(Pituruna)_** and  
(3) Towards Rishis **_(Rishi Runa)._** 

Subsequently **Maharishi** **Vyasa,** who is regarded as **Adi Guru** and as the greatest intellect, who classified the **Four Vedas** and who is also the author of the **Great Epic Mahabharatha,** which includes the **Bhagavad Gita,** considered that the three pious obligations were not complete.  

He added the Fourth (4) pious obligation namely towards **Human Society (_Manavaruna)._** 

The inclusion of the Fourth pious obligation is the most remarkable contribution of **Maharishi Vyasa.** In view of his being the ‘**_Ocean of Knowledge’_** and on account of the supreme intellectual heights he reached, the nation pays obeisance to him down to this day by observing ‘**Vyasa Poornima’** as **_‘Gurupoornima’._** I am giving below the relevant verses from the Mahabharatha, in which these **four pious obligations** have been indicated in **_Adi Parva:_**

Rishi Shadamarshana belonged to the Vishnuvrida Gana. Alavandar says that Nadamuni was born in Shadamarshana Gothra and travelled throughout India to spread the timeless message of Sanatana Dharma. Kotikanyakadhanam Lakshmikumara Thathadesikan who was the Acharya of Venkatapathy, the ruler Vijayanagar, renovated several important shrines hailed by the ALWARS and thus nurtured and sustained Hinduism. 

For centuries, the noble descendants of this family learnt the Vedas, followed the rituals enjoined upon them and became famous for propagating SHRI VAISHNAVA SAMPRADAYA AND VEDANTA. Mahavidwan U Ve Agnihotram Ramanuja Thathachariar belongs to this glorious tradition. He has mastered the Vedas, Smritis, Kalpa Sutras, Ithihasas, Puranas and the two Mimamsas. He was born as the son of Shri Thathadesika and Alamelumanga. He learnt the Vedas from his elder Uncle Srinivasacharyar Swami known as Pattuswami and his younger Uncle Gopala Desikacharyar. From a very young age, Agnihotram Swami has dedicated himself to the spread of Sanatana Dharma in this land.

In the massive effort that preceded the incorporation of the **_‘freedom of religion’_** provisions in the Constitution, men of eminence and stature were involved in various ways and at different levels. Notable among those who were actively associated with that endeavour was **Mahavidwan Shri. U.ve. Agnihotram Ramanuja Thathachariar.** In the course of this mission and endeavour, he met the members of a Parliamentary delegation from Great Britain in 1946 when they were on a visit to Madras **_(to whom he was introduced by Kasturi Srinivasan, Editor of The Hindu)_** and, on their suggestion, he submitted a memorandum to them at Delhi. 

Four years earlier, **Agnihotram Swami** had sought an appointment with **Sir Strafford Cripps** in 1942 to argue out the case for the sustenance of **Sanatana Dharma** in India. Animated by the same spirit, he appeared before the Constituent Assembly Committee in 1947 and spoke passionately in favour of Constitutional safeguards for the protection of Hindu Religion and Hindu Religious Institutions. As the Secretary of the All India Committee of Math Heads, he organized the National Sammelan of Heads of Math for bringing about national integration on three occasions.

Coming as he does in a distinguished line of _acharya purushas_, Agnihotram Thathachariar performs what is known in Vaishnavite spiritual parlance as ‘Pancha Samskaram’. He has his own circle of ardent disciples. In him is seen a rare blend of Himalayan erudition in the Vedas and other scriptures and expertise in the performance of yagas and yagnas sanctioned by them. It is not therefore surprising that he has been on great demand from across the country for delivering lectures, participating in seminars or conducting yagas. He has personally supervised and conducted more than 300 Soma Yagas all over India.

Agnihotram Thathachariar has been running a campaign, so to say, for a scientific approach to religion. 

_In the West, he says, religion has always had its appeal as a subject of study and the universities in those countries have always encouraged even the study of the Vedas and the Puranas. Universities in the West have also made concerted attempts to probe the significance of the Vedic rituals and the atmosphere in those Western Universities is such as to facilitate the growth of scientific temper vis-à-vis religion._

Agnihotram Swami’s publications are many. ‘Yaskar Nirukthi’ is the base for any modern scholar to understand the hidden meaning of Vedic texts. His Tamil translation of ‘Yaskar Nirukthi’ has been hailed as a great work. Another great work is ‘**Eternal Relevance of Vedas’,** a work in English published by **Tirumalai-Tirupathi Devasthanam** some years ago. He has surveyed the democratic spirit, traditions and practices in the Vedic era in his Sanskrit work **‘_Vedakalina Janathantra Sthanani’._** In his book **‘Azhwars and Vedas’,** he has explained how the mystic Azhwars of Tamilnadu were deeply rooted in the Vedas and Vedic Culture. His other works like **‘Women in Vedas’** and **‘God loves you; you love God’ are great masterpieces.**

Mahavidwan U Ve Agnihotram Ramanuja Thathachariar has been the moving spirit behind all the 10 Vedic Sammelans conducted by Yogakshema Trust during the last 16 years. 

Emerson said 150 years ago that _‘A great institution is the lengthened shadow of one man’._+++(5)+++ If that be so, Yogakshema Trust is indeed the lengthened shadow of Agnihotram Ramanuja Thathachariar.

Dr. Nagaswami, the former Director of Archaelogy in the Government of Tamilnadu has paid this Centenary tribute to **Agnihotram Swami: 

> ‘Most outstanding authority on Indian thought and religion today. He is a master of Vedas, Agamas (Temple rituals), Tamil Hymns and Prabhandam literature. In spite of being an outstanding scholar, deeply rooted in traditional systems of learning, he is refreshingly an original thinker with a modern scientific outlook. 
>
> He is a very good historian of Indian religion – its evolution across centuries and its current state. He is not a conservative personality with a closed mind. He can stand up to any international scholar, with an uncanny ability and sensibility to discuss with him on equal terms in an absolutely modern and disciplined academic manner, about various aspects of most ancient Vedic sacrificial rituals and procedures. 
>
> He has been chief advisor and priest to several Hindu organizations in regard to many lost and forgotten Vedic sacrifices in different parts of India. Many international scholars from all parts of the World have sought his advice and guidance in understanding the complicated ramifications relating to the letter and spirit of the Hindu ethos. He will never hesitate to criticise orthodoxy if it is not acceptable to modern enquiries. 
>
> His Holiness Paramacharya of Kanchi made him the Chief Supervisor and guide for organizing the Veda Agama Silpa Sadas at Ilayathangudi in 1961 in which he kept a large public audience spell-bound through the brilliant manner in which he summarized the entire proceedings for 12 days. I had the unique privilege of witnessing his intuitive and unsurpassed pedagogic public performance on that sacred occasion’.
>
> WHAT IS THE LESSON THAT ALL OF US CAN LEARN FROM THE GLORIOUS LIFE OF AGNIHOTRAM SWAMI_? I can only put it in the words of another great saint of modern India_: ‘Our existence as embodied beings is purely momentary; what are a hundred years in eternity? But if we shatter the chains of egotism, and melt into the ocean of humanity, we share its dignity. To feel that we are something is to set up a barrier between God and ourselves; to cease feeling that we are something is to become one with God. A drop in the ocean partakes of the greatness of its parent, although it is unconscious of it. But it is dried up as soon as it enters upon an existence independent of the ocean. As soon as we become one with the ocean in the shape of God, there is no more rest for us, nor indeed do we need rest any longer. Our very sleep is action. For we sleep with the thought of God in our hearts. This restlessness constitutes true rest. This never-ceasing agitation holds the key to peace ineffable. This supreme state of total surrender is difficult to describe, but not beyond the bounds of human experience. It has been attained by many dedicated souls, and may be attained by ourselves as well_’._

Let us all offer our reverential and humble salutations to this great Maharishi in the decadent India of today.
