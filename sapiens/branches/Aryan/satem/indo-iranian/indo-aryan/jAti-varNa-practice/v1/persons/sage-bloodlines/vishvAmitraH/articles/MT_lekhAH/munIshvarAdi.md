## Family
The medieval mathematician-scientist gives his genealogy going back several generations, with his family graced by several scholars (shown). The had their origin in an unidentified town of ga~ngAbhaushila on the banks of the godAvarI river. From there, they moved at chintAmani's time to the village of dadhigrAma (modern Dahigaon in Vidarbha, Mh state). The astronomer ballAla migrated to kAshI, evidently during the relief offered to H by the Mogol tyrant Akbar. His son kR^iShNa, a algebraist did some astrological (daivaj~na) consultation for the Mogol tyrant Jahangir, who gave him some concessions despite being a H.  He received his mathematical training under viShNu, a member of the academic lineage of the famous mathematician gaNesha daivaj~na. 

munIshvara also known as vishvarUpa studied under his cousin, kR^iShNa's son nArAyaNa, thus linking his familial lineage with the academic lineage of gaNesha daivaj~na. He wrote a treatise on the portable cylindrical sundial created by gaNesha, which was a fusion of the H pratoda-yantra and Mogol astronomical instrumentation. munIshvara briefly provided astrological consultation for the tyrant Shah Jahan, probably in connection with his declaring himself Sāhib-i Qirān-i Sānī upon the conjunction of Jupiter and Saturn. In this context, he uses the tyrants invasion of Balkh as a metaphor in one of his verses.

## ranganAtha's son
Imagine you have just finished writing your book, and at the same time, a son who would grow to be a great intellectual was born to you. That is what happened to the Yajurvedin Raṅganātha, a Vidarbhan astronomer living in Kāśī. He expresses the same in a Bhujaṅgaprayāta verse:

śake tattva-tithy unmite caitra-māse site śambhu-tithyāṃ budhe 'rkodayān me ।  
dalāḍhya-dvi-nārāca-nāḍīṣu jātau munīśārka-siddhānta-gūḍha-prakāśau ॥

In the Śaka year measured by the tattvas (25) and the tithis (15) [Śaka 1525 = 1603 CE], in the bright fortnight of the month of Caitra, on the eleventh (14th according to Ramasubramanian) lunar day (Śambhu-tithi) on a Wednesday, at ten (dvi-nārāca = 2 x 5) and a half nāḍī-s [1 nāḍī= 24 min; 4 hours and 12 minutes] after sunrise: To me were born a son Munīśvara and the commentary Gūḍhārthaprakāśa on the Arka-siddhānta [Sūryasiddhānta].

They belong to an illustrious medieval family of scholars
