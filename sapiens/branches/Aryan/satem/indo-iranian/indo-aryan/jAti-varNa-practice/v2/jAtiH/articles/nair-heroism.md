When a temple in Kollam (Venad/Travancore) was plundered by the Portuguese in the 16th century, the Nairs charged at them to defend the temple, without ever giving a thought about their own lives. They gladly embraced their deaths in front of far numerous enemy forces.

> As the troops began to march, a Nair wearing golden armlets and earrings came out to engage them, carrying his sword and leather shield (adarga), accompanied by twelve or fifteen other well-dressed men, also with swords and shields. The leader charged our men with such courage and boldness as someone seeking death, and all the rest did the same. However, in a short space of time, they were all killed, fighting with great effort throughout without ever backing down.This Nair, who voluntarily surrendered himself to death, was one of the jangadas [sworn guardians/captains] of that temple. For it is customary for kings and great lords who have temples in their lands to have them guarded by two captains—honorable and brave men—along with troops assigned to them, whom they call jangadas. These men serve as advisors and ministers of the temple’s affairs, funded out of its revenues, and the king or lord of the land replaces them at will.
  
> Of these jangadas of the Nairs, Sabellicus [Marcantonio Sabellico] also makes mention, and says that the Nairs of Malabar maintained the order of chivalry, walking along the roads to defend noblewomen and maidens and redressing their grievances.
