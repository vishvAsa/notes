title = "Suyya"

Source: [TW](https://x.com/Fintech03/status/2082278683604373985)

A forgotten genius saved Kashmir from a drowning death, by tossing a king's fortune straight into a roaring river.

In the 9th century, catastrophic earthquakes caused massive landslides near Yakshadara (modern day Baramulla), choking the narrow gorge of the Vitasta (Jhelum) River.

The entire Kashmir Valley turned into a submerged swamp. Agriculture collapsed & a khari (unit) of grain soared to an astronomical 1050 gold coins, causing widespread famine.

While the king’s ministers held endless debates & offered prayers, an unschooled man of humble origins named Suyya began publicly claiming: "I have the knowledge to free the land from the water, but I have no means."

When King Avantivarman heard of Suyya’s claims, he summoned him to court. When asked what resources he required to tame a mighty river, Suyya did not ask for armies/heavy iron tools, he asked for pots of gold coins from the royal treasury.

The royal court erupted in laughter. Courtiers dismissed him as an unhinged, eccentric madman, asking what money could possibly do against a roaring river.

King Avantivarman, however, decided to take a gamble & granted him the funds.

Suyya took the pots of gold, boarded a boat & traveled directly to the landslide site at Yakshadara where the river was choked with mud & massive stone boulders.

Instead of making a speech/trying to manually dig, Suyya did something that made the onlookers certain he was insane: he began grabbing handfuls of gold coins & tossing them directly into the roaring, mud-choked riverbed.

The news spread like wildfire through surrounding villages. 1000s of impoverished, starving locals rushed to the site & jumped into the freezing river. To find & drag out the gold coins buried beneath the silt, the crowd pulled out 100s of heavy boulders, logs & debris choking the riverbed.

Within days, the crowd had cleared the natural dam. The bottleneck opened, the Jhelum rushed through & the submerged valley drained out completely.

Suyya was not finished. Once the water receded, he executed a masterplan that transformed Kashmir’s geography: He built a stone dam to temporarily hold the river, cleaned out the remaining silt & constructed stone embankments along a 42 mile stretch.

He changed the junction of the Vitasta (Jhelum) & Sindh rivers to flow directly into Wular Lake, turning marshlands into fertile agricultural soil.

Kalhana records that Suyya collected soil samples from different villages, measured the exact time each sample took to dry & engineered customized irrigation channels for each village based on soil retention rates.

The impact was immediate, the price of grain crashed overnight from 1050 gold coins to just 36 coins per khari.

King Avantivarman showered him with honors & the town of Sopore in Jammu & Kashmir stands today on the site of the ancient city founded in his honor.... Suyyapura (Modern day Sopore).
