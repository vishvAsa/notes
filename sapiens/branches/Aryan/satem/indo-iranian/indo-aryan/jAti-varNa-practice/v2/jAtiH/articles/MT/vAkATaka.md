The dynastic name vAkATaka is believed to have been derived from the place vAkATa. However, the identity of that place remains an enduring mystery to this date.

> wouldn’t that be baghāṭa village, niwāɽī dist. in bundélkhaṇḍa region in m.p.

That is phonetically a good choice but the geographic evidence would favor something in the vidarbha region where they seemed to have first emerged; this would be a bit too north for that.

> I thought it was almost settled that pravarasena I had kañcanā, nācanā in pannā dist. as its capital in todays m.p. many early vākāṭaka records had also been found in pannā. niwāɽī dist. is ~ 200 km north-west to pannā.

Interestingly, Mirashi IIRC thought pravarasena-I never crossed north of Narmada and centered him more to the south. He also identified the first reference to a vAkATaka in prAkR^ita form at amarAvati which is considerably south of the proposed place but closer to their center of gravity. In light of the doubt cast on Paunar being pravarapura, I have also begun wondering about what was the real vAkATa -- I'm less convinced of the popular proposal.
