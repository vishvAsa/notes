About 1,100 years ago, an Arabic travel book listed what Indians counted as devotion. 3 entries, all within 3 pages.

Inns on the highways, built for the accommodation of travellers, with dealers set up so passengers could buy what they happened to want.

Rice. When a wandering ascetic, pressed by hunger, stopped at a door, the people inside brought out rice immediately and joyfully, believing there was great merit in doing so. The man ate and withdrew, never returning to ask again unless need forced him.

And on empty seas, men of piety sought unknown or newly discovered islands, planted coconut trees and sank wells of water for the use of ships that sailed to those parts.

Food for a stranger, a roof for a traveller, water for a sailor. Indian hospitality documented 1,100 years ago. 🙏🙏

Source: Abu Zayd al-Sirafi, Ancient Accounts of India and China, pp. 87-89, Renaudot's 1733 English translation.
