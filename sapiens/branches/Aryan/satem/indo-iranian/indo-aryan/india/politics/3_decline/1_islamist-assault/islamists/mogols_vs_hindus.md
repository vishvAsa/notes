## Akbar
Hindu proclivities were a peculiarity of Akbar as a person and certainly not generalizable to the Mughals as a whole.

## Jahāngīr
Jahāngīr was also somewhat tolerant, barring a few isolated events (e.g. desecration of the Varāha temple at Puṣkara, banishment of "Seora" Jains from Gujarat and destruction of their temples, execution of the Sikh Guru Arjan, etc.), 


## Šāh Jahān
yet Šāh Jahān was an orthodox Muslim and his reign marked an ideological shift towards orthodox Sunni Islam:

Šāh Jahān explicitly banned the construction and repair of Hindu temples in accordance with the šarīʿah ruling regarding ḏimmīs. Temples which were recently built during the reign of Akbar or were still under construction in Vārāṇasī or any other districts of the protected dominions (mamālik-i maḥrūsa) were demolished.

The provincial chronicler (waqāʾiʿ-nigār, lit. "recorder of events") of Allāhābād reported seventy-six temples in the region of Banāras had been leveled to the ground.


Šāh Jahān ordered the destruction of temples near Achwal (modern Achabal/Achival) in Kashmir as the site was regularly frequented by Hindū pilgrims. He had the site completely uprooted, and renamed the "abode of infidelity" ("kufrābād") as Islamabad.

Šāh Jahān ordered the destruction of a "great or lofty temple" built by Raja Bir Singh Deo in Orchha and had a mosque erected in its place as a religious and political statement in response to the Bundela rebellion.

Hindu husbands who had married Muslim women in Rajauri, Bhimbar, parts of Gujarat, etc. were given a strict ultimatum to convert to Islam or face severe penalties.

He replaced the sijdah practice introduced by Akbar with the čahār taslīm as he firmly believed that prostration should only belong to Allāh.
