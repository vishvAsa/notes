title = "Tuglaq bride demand"
+++


While it's true that Fīrōz Šāh Tuġluq's mother was Punjabi, it was actually sipahsālār Rajab (i.e. Sulṭān Ġiyāṯu d-Dīn Tuġluq's younger brother), a Turk of K͟hurāsān, who insisted on marrying the daughter of Rāṇā Mal (= Malla?), the Bhaṭṭī Rājpūt ruler of Depālpur.

Rāṇā Mal insulted and abused Ġiyāṯu d-Dīn's envoys, so Ġiyāṯu d-Dīn entered Rāṇā Mal's talwaṇḍī or farm settlement and demanded an entire year's land tax from the landlords and chieftains, which caused the people extreme distress and forced Rāṇā Mal to consent and give his daughter's hand in marriage to Rajab.

The text says that Rāṇā Mal's daughter was named "Nāʾilah" and that Ġiyāṯu d-Dīn renamed her as Kadbānū ("Lady of the House," from Middle Persian kadag-bānūg), but "Nāʾilah" is an Arabic name and it's highly unlikely that the daughter of a 14th century Hindu chieftain would bear an Arabic name.

Nāʾilah is likely a phonetic approximation/adaptation of an unknown Apabhraṃśa or Old Punjabi name.

Alternatively, it may be a literary invention (i.e. they may've supplied her with a conventional Muslim name because her indigenous name was no longer remembered).
