Source: TW

Pakistan was founded as a sort of “successor state” to the Mughals and Delhi Sultanate, so Pakistanis have inherited their biases.

Slave race

“From this, understand the relation between the Turk and the Hindu.
The Turk is like a lion, the Hindu like a deer.
Even if the deer shows boldness to another deer, what heart remains when the lion shows its lion-nature?
In form, an elephant is no lesser than a lion, but before the lion even the elephant cannot muster courage.
The custom estabished by the wheel (of the sky) is that the very existence of Hindus is for the sake of Turks.
For the Turk dominates them whenever he strives:
He seizes them, crushes them, and sells them.
As the Hindu is a slave however he may be, no one exerts force against his own servant.
May God preserve that master who cherishes his slave.
One should not cast sharp glances at that goat who is raised for the sole purpose of being eaten.
What need is there for sharpening the dagger against one who dies just from a mere glance?”

  • Nuh Sipihr, ʾAmīr K͟husraw Dihlavī https://x.com/Saatvata/status/2094856673626034520

Military traits

Some of the nobles said:

“By the favour of Almighty God we may conquer India, but if we establish ourselves permanently therein, our race will degenerate, and our children will become like the natives of those regions, and in a few generations their strength and valour will diminish.”

The commanders of regiments were disturbed at these words, but I (Timur) said to them:

“My object in the invasion of Hindustan is to lead an expedition against the infidels that, according to the law of Mohammed (upon whom and his family be the blessing and peace of God!), we may convert the people of that country to the true faith and purify the land itself from infidelity and polytheism, and that we may overthrow their temples and idols and become conquerors and crusaders before God.” They gave an unwilling consent, but I placed no reliance upon them. - ʾAmīr Tīmūr Image

“The Turani people have ever been soldiers. They are very expert in making charges, raids, night-attacks and arrests.
They feel no suspicion, despair or shame when commanded to make a retreat in the very midst of a fight, which means, in other words, ‘drawing the arrow back’; and they are a hundred stages remote from the crass stupidity of the Hindustanis, who would part with their heads but not leave their positions [in battle].
In every way, you should confer favours on this race, because on many occasions these men can do the necessary service, when no other race can.” - Awrangzēb ʿĀlamgīr

Cunning persians

Without [being charged with] prejudice and enmity, we may say that as the Sun is the guardian planet of the Persians, the intellectual keenness of those men in quickness of perception and foresight is four times as great as that of the Indians, whose tutelary planet is Saturn.
Their only defect is that by reason of its conjunction with Venus, they have grown ease-loving, whereas men governed by Saturn are accustomed to toil; and the nearness of Saturn to Jupiter is really more frequent [than that of the Sun to Venus?].
But there is a little natural inferiority and meanness in Saturn, the exceptions being certain individuals only, in whose horoscopes some other planet is their helper.
The purport of my words is this, that you should be on your guard against the great cunning of the Persians and never submit to me such [seemingly] friendly overtures, as they only prove your lack of sagacity. (Verse)
The flood kissed the foot of the wall only to overthrow it!" - Awrangzēb ʿĀlamgīr

Pro-turani bias

“The majority of nobles at the court of Akbar were Turanis.
Turani was a term applied to any person coming from north of Oxus (central Asia) where the Turkish language were spoken.
They belonged to the sunni sect that was more dominant in India. Since they were from the reigning dynasty they came to be regarded as their kith and kin.
In the initial phase, atleast, it appears that for this reason they were particularly well treated by the Mughal rulers in India.
The majority of the Turanis had been in the service of the Mughal rulers for generations.
As a result they looked towards the Mughal Court for patronage. On their part the Mughal Emperors had a sense of gratitude toward the Turanis. Babur invited them to come and settle here. He sent letters in all directions saying we shall take into full favour all who enter our service and especially such as served our father, grandfather and ancestor. If such will come to us, they will receive fitting benefits, whoever there may be of the families of Sahib-i-Quran and Chingiz Khan, let them turn out to our court.
The most high has given us sovereignty in Hindustan, let them come so that we may see prosperity together. Humayun, while planning the reconquest of India, invited a number of Turanis to join his ranks.”

“Since the ruling family was of Turani orgins, the Turani nobles were large in number. For instance. the Turani nobles who accompanied Babur to Hindustan were predominantly of Turani origin with the exception of a few individuals of Khurasani and Persian Origin, who might have joined his service after the termination of Timuride rule in Hirat.”

Color bias

While color bias existed to prior to Islamic conquest (e.g. in the description of King Vena in the Purāṇas and descriptions of Bhilla hunters), Classical Sanskrit, Prakrit, Tamil, Kannada, etc. literature often portrays dark/dusky skin (śyāmā/śyāmalāṅgī) as an admirable feature in a woman.

For example, in the description of Draupadī.

Following the Turkic conquest, dusky/dark (“crow-like,” “tar-like,” “naphtha-like”) skin became a visual shorthand for foreignness, political subjugation, and religious infidelity:

“The women [of Kashmir] especially are very handsome and it is from this country that, nearly every individual, when first admitted to the court of the Great Mogul, selects wives or concubines, that his children may be whiter than the Indians and pass for genuine Moguls.” - François Bernier

Ḥasan Niẓāmī

“The dark-skinned Hindus had taken up position on the highway. They could outstrip onager and deer in speed, and in making an onslaught on the enemy they were faster than wind. It appeared as if they were demons who had besmeared their faces with ink and soot, or they were sentinels of hell with their visages blackened with tar and pitch.”

“The (mirror holder) of the sun pulled out a bright dagger from the sheath of the blue dome (sky) and showed forth a shining scimitar from behind this revolving shield The sun-lit candle of the chess-players thrown away this deceitful dome and burnt down the crow faced Hindus in the night like a moth.
It illuminated the world by showing its face which was fair like that of a denizen of Rum.
By the laughter of the Turk of day-break it converted the oyster of the earth into a casket of pearls.”

“The head of the arrow acquired the colour of large size ruby from the blood smeared bodies of the wounded, and the tip of the spear became yellow like amber due to the reflection of pale faces.
It seemed as if the dagger of destruction had sifted the saw-dust of Zareer (a yellow dying wood) on the swarthy faces of Hindus, and the hand of death had sprinkled saffron on their pitch-like bodies.
Jatwan (Chetwan), who was the cause of mischief and disturbance and the root of infidelity and malice, became a companion of anxiety and an associate of remorse.”

“With the battle-axe of crescent he pushed the moon of the lives of his enemies into the straits of zaiq (one of the mansions of the moon).
The bodies of the warning lions raised aloft by his spear-head looked like chickens dangling from the spit.
He caused sieve-like-holes to appear on the surface of the sheaths of his enemies by his javelins that shot like stars and struck like thunder-bolts. Instead of collar of servitude he threw the noose of his lasso which is like the necklace of the Gemini, round the necks of the warriors arranged against him.
He used the jet-black bodies of Hindus as a sheath for his blood stained sword that flashed like sunbeams.
Like the first rays of the dawn, he captured the world with his Mars-like dagger.”

“There were so many huge elephants that the level ground assumed the shape of lofty mountains. The pitch black faces of Hindus turned the surface of waterless deserts into lakes of tar. Roads and thoroughfares were jam-packed with piles of weapons and countless heads of cattle. All over the world one could see signs of elegant houses and attractive inns. The news of his illustrious deeds and glorious achievements spread to the remote parts of the world and was heard by all mortals.” - Tājul-Maʾās̱ir, Ḥasan Niẓāmī
