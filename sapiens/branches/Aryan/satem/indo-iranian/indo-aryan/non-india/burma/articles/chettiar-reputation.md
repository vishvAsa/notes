Burmese people generally aren't fond of Indians/South Asians.

This dislike is not limited to Rohingyas and Bengalis.

Nattukottai Chettiars (essentially Tamil Baniyas) were vilified as "rapacious usurers" and "fiery dragons" (in the words of a Karen representative to the 1929 Burma Provincial Banking Enquiry) who drained Burma's wealth.

This is because the British bankers who were sitting in corporate offices in Rangoon refused to loan to Burmese farmers because they regarded the loans as unmanageable (clearing malaria-ridden jungles and swamps in the Irawaddy Delta to build rice paddies was incredibly an risky venture), which left the Chettiars as the most "affordable" option for peasants, giving the Chettiars a virtual monopoly on rural credit.

A large percentage of the agricultural loans in Burma ended up in default, so the Chettiars baked in a "risk premium" into their rates.
The interest rate could therefore be as high as 25%.

The was slightly lower than the interest rate offered by Chinese merchants (generally around 30-35% per year) and was more organized and transparent than the exploitative sabape loans of native Burmese lenders, yet it was still very extortionist from the perspective of the average Burmese peasant.

They don't remember the Chettiars for financing the growth of Burma's rice economy during the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries and making Burma one of the world's leading rice exporters by the early twentieth century.

They only remember the Chettiars for setting high interest rates and "stealing" land from native Burmese peasants when they defaulted on their loans during the Great Depression.

As a consequence, they were viewed with similar stereotypes as Jews and this stereotype later extended to other South Asians.
