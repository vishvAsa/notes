Hindu Dharmaśāstras and Dharmasūtras prohibit sapiṇḍa and sagotra marriages, i.e. marriage with someone who is related within seven generations paternally or five generations maternally.
In the modern Hindu Marriage Act of 1955 (which governs Hindus, Buddhists, Jains, and Sikhs in India), this prohibition has been relaxed to five generations paternally and three generations maternally, unless local custom allows it.
The Kaḷaṣa likewise have a prohibition on intermarriage within seven generations paternally and four generations maternally.
The former prohibition (prohibition on intermarriage within seven generations paternally) may plausibly be reconstructed for the Proto-Indo-Iranian culture as it is found even among the Nuristanis.
There is no strong reason to assume that the Yaz Culture or Early Iranics were too different from their precursors.
