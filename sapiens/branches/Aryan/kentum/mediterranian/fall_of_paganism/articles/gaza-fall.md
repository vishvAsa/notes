I thought it was well known that Gaza had been a thriving center of Hellenistic culture (not just neo-Platonism) from the time of Alexander up until Christianization in the 5th and 6th centuries AD.

It was also arguably one of the last strongholds of paganism in the Levant and the Roman Empire as a whole, so much so that Jerome referred to Gaza as an "urbs gentīlium" or a "city of gentiles/heathens."
Even in pre-Hellenic times it was a Philistine and Canaanite city and only sporadically fell under Jewish sovereignty.

Gaza was so thoroughly pagan that even Emperor Arcadius and John of Caesarea seem to have been initially hesitant to undertake forceful Christianization of Gaza.

When Porphrius requested permission to extirpate paganism, John of Caesarea argued that the time was not appropriate since the Winter Solstice was fast approaching but "Saint" Porphyrius was not going to accept any such excuse.

"Saint" Porphyrius stated:

ἐὰν θέλῃ ἡμᾶς ὁ Θεὸς εἶναι καὶ τὰ πλήθη Γάζης ἐπιστρέψαι καὶ ἐν χειμῶνι δυνατός ἐστιν σῶσαι

"eàn thélēi hēmâs ho Theòs eînai kaì tá plḗthē Gádzēs epistrépsai kaì en kheimō̂ni dunatós estin sō̂sai"

"If God wills that we endure and turn the multitudes of Gaza [unto Christ], He is able to save us even in the middle of winter."

John had no choice but to concede, stating "may the will of Christ be done!" (tò thélēma toû Khristoû genēthḗtō").

So Porphrius and John of Caesarea go to Procopius, who leads them to John Chrysostom (the Archbishop of Constantinople, the seat of the Eastern Roman Empire).

Chrysostom introduces them to Empress Augusta Eudoxia's chamberlain, who was named Amantios.
Amantios spoke with Eudoxia on behalf of Porphyrius and John of Caesarea. 

Eudoxia called them forward the next day and sought their blessings, stating that she already knew why they were there and that she would speak to the Emperor on their behalf.

The Emperor was not at al enthralled by Eudoxia's request, stating:

οἶδα ὅτι ἡ πόλις ἐκείνη κατείδωλός ἐστιν, ἀλλ᾽ εὐγνωμονεῖ περὶ τὴν εἰσφορὰν τῶν δημοσίων πολλὰ συντελοῦσα. Ἐὰν οὖν αἰφνιδιάσωμεν αὐτοὺς τῷ φόβῳ, φυγῇ χρήσονται καὶ ἀπόλλυμεν τοσοῦτον κανόνα.

oîda hóti hē pólis ekeínē kateídōlós estin, all' eugnōmoneî perì tḕn eisphoràn tō̂n dēmosíōn pollà sunteloûsa. Eàn oûn aiphnidiásōmen autoùs tō̂i phóbōi, phugē̂i khrḗsontai kaì apóllomen tosoûton kanóna.

"I know well that this city is filled with idols, but it is willing to pay taxes and is bringing in much contribution. If we suddenly surprise them with terror, they will flee and we will lose much tax revenue"

Emperor Arcadius preferred to "wean the Gazans off" from their idolatry via gradual persecution (stripping pagans of their ranks, closing down temples, etc.)

Yet Eudoxia overruled her husband's objection, stating:

Ὁ κύριος ἔχει βοηθῆσαι τοῖς δούλοις αὐτοῦ τοῖς Χριϲτιανοῖς, κἂν θέλωμεν ἡμεῖς κἂν μὴ θέλωμεν.

ho kúrios ékhei boēthē̂sai toîs doúlois autoû toîs Khristianoîs, kàn théllōmen hēmeîs kàn mḕ théllōmen.

"Whether we like it or not, the Lord will assist his Christians as they are his servants."

Eudoxia entrusted Porphrius and John of Caesarea with "wondrous" man named Cynegius who was "burning with zeal for the faith":

προσκαλεσαμένη δὲ αὐτὸν ἡ αὐγούστα παρήγγειλεν αὐτῷ πάντα τὰ εἰδωλεῖα ἕως ἐδάφους καταστρέψαι καὶ πυρὶ παραδοῦναι.

proskalesaménē dè autòn hē Aúgousta parḗngeilen autō̂i pánta tà eidōleîa héōs edáphous katastrépsai kaì purì paradoûnai.

Summoning him (i.e. Cynegius), Augusta (i.e. empress Eudoxia) commanded him to tear all the idol temples down to their foundations and commit them to fire.

"Saint" Porphyrius and his posse went to work demolishing pagan temples and the current Church of Porphyrius stands atop the site of the pagan Marneion, built in honor of the deity Zeus Marnas.
