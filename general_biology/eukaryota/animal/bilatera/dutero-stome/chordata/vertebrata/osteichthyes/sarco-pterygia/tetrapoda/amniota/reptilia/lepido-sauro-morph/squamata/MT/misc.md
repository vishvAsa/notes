## Bellows
Varanus lizard hunting. Most ordinary lizards face a constraint that prevents them from running faster than a certain speed: they use the same muscles to run and breathe, so fast movement limits airflow. However, Varanus lizards have a pump in their throat, the gular pump that acts like bellows to keep up their breathing efficiency while sprinting: that's how you can see it keep up with a mammal. Thus, it is likely its breathing capacity converged on some Mesozoic basal archosaurs with similar hunting strategies.

## Tail shedding
Source: [TW](https://x.com/blog_supplement/status/2114034513072587012)

While it might seem extreme, we can conclude that the lizard shedding its tail is an evolutionarily stable strategy. 2/3rds of the lizard families are capable of sacrificing their tail to escape and regrow it later. These include lacertoids, scincids, geckos and, to some extent, agamids. This fossil from the Myanmar amber of 99 million years ago suggests that lizards have been shedding their tails for at least that long. By evolutionary inference, it was obviously much longer, because by 99 Mya the lizards had already radiated into most of these families, and the common ancestor might be inferred to have had this trait.
