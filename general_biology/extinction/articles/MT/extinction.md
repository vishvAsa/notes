## Mars
The images from the rover of the rivers running across the surface of Mars are hauntingly familiar. If context was lacking, I would easily take it for a terrestrial scene of a dried-up river from a desertified land with haze from a sandstorm. It also seems like it was indeed once dotted with early life, much like Earth, which has now probably become extinct over all or much of the planet. Like the pArIkShita-s of hastinApura who once went extinct. It raises a philosophical question of whether there is anything to life at all if its ultimate destination is such extinction. Or are we ourselves descendants of life once shared by Mars and Earth...

## Asteroid impact
Source: [TW](https://x.com/blog_supplement/status/2111860123098350054)

The Manicouagan crater (Canada, 214-215 million years ago) and Popigai crater (Russia, 35.7 Mya) were caused by impacts of asteroids about 5 km in diameter. There is no evidence that those two impacts caused any large-scale extinction event. The Chicxulub crater (Yucatan, 66 Mya), caused by a 10-15 km impactor, caused a mass extinction. Assuming comparable density and velocity, the kinetic energy of the latter would be 8-25 times greater than that of the other two. That seems to give a threshold at which an asteroid impact would cause global extinction.

Based on the power-law distribution, there is a formula that has been reported for the time interval of such events:
$T
= 5 \times 10^5 \times D^{2.5}$, where
$D
$ is the diameter of the asteroid in km.

If this formula approximates reality, a Chicxulub-type impact will occur once in $\approx 158$ My – an extremely rare event. However, a 100-meter impactor is estimated to occur approximately every 1500 years. One such hit could devastate a city.
