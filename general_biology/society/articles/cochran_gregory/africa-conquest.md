Tropical diseases were, of course, the primary reason that Europeans could not conquer much of Africa in those days.  when they had means of protection ( mainly quinine) , they conquered Africa, pronto. 
Africans were genetically resistant to  falciparum malaria, sleeping sickness, and yellow fever: Europeans were not.

Joao de Barros, a Portuguese historian of the sixteenth century, said, "But it seems that for our sins, or for some inscrutable judgment of God, in all the entrances of this great Ethiopia we navigate along, He has placed a striking angel with a flaming sword of deadly fevers, who prevents us from penetrating into the interior to the springs of this garden, whence proceed these rivers of gold that flow to the sea in so many parts of our conquest."
