Although it's true that India and China together produced around half of the world's total GDP for most of recorded history, this observation is not as meaningful as it sounds.
Historical GDP reconstructions of economies prior to the Industral Revolution lean heavily on estimates of agricultural output/consumption.
India and China could support far more people due to greater availability of arable land and areas with climate suitable for rice cultivation.
Not only does rice provide more calories per hectare than wheat, millet, barley, etc., but wet rice fields self-fertilize (i.e. they can receive nitrogen through biological fixation, sediment and irrigation water), so farmers in India and China typically didn't have to leave a third of their fields fallow to recover like European farmers did.
It's only natural that this comparatively higher agricultural output would also reflect in the aggregate GDP.
