**That waterproof liquid mixed into the concrete helps a lot, but by itself it will NOT give you a 300-year flat roof - it will give you a 50+ year slab that still needs a membrane and tiles on top.**

What they sell here as "waterproof liquid" is an integral admixture. The common one you see on Bengaluru sites is Dr Fixit Pidiproof LW+.

### What it actually is

It's not magic waterproof paint. It is a specially formulated integral liquid waterproofing compound composed of surface-active plasticizing agents, polymers and additives. It improves workability of freshly mixed concrete and improves waterproofing of concrete. Dose is 200 ml per 50 kg bag of cement.

It does 3 things inside the slab:

1. **Plasticizes** - lets you use less water for same slump, so concrete becomes denser.
2. **Blocks pores** - polymer particles fill capillary pores.
3. **Corrosion delay** - it resists corrosion of steel and improves longevity.

Better versions are crystalline admixtures (Caltite, Krysta Leakproof, Xypex). Those grow crystals inside when water enters, so they self-heal hairline cracks up to 0.4mm.

### How long does it last?

Good news: unlike a sheet membrane that degrades in sun, an admixture does not suffer the drawbacks of a waterproof membrane, which can be prone to in-service damage and limited service life owing to natural degradation by UV and weathering. It protects concrete from within, and extends the lifespan.

Realistic service life from data:

- Bare RCC flat slab in Karnataka, no admixture, no membrane: 25-35 years to first major leak / spall
- RCC + LW+ type plasticizer only: 40-50 years
- RCC + crystalline admixture + good curing: 50+ years of integral protection, pore blocking admixtures offer a permanent solution that is integrated into the mix

Why it still leaks eventually:

- It cannot bridge a structural crack >0.5mm from thermal movement. Tirupati roof surface hits 65C in May. Slab expands, cracks at corners.
- It does not give you slope. If water ponds for 48 hours, it will eventually find a path through construction joints.
- Labour in farmhouse sites often adds extra water to make concrete flow. That wipes out all benefit of LW+. You need slump <120mm.

### If you want to use it for your 3-story farmhouse, do it like this

This is the cheapest way to get close to 100 years with a flat look:

**Mix:** M25 minimum, not M20. 1 cement : 1.3 sand (M-sand washed) : 2.5 jelly (12mm). Water-cement ratio 0.45 max. This is more important than the liquid.

**Admixture:** Don't use only LW+. Use crystalline admixture. Eg Fosroc Conplast WL Xtra or Dr Fixit Krysta Leakproof. It is designed for roof slabs, basements, water tanks. Dose as per company, typically 2% by weight of cement. If you only get LW+, use it, but add it in mixing water, not after.

**Pour:** Pour full slab in one day, no cold joints. Vibrate properly. If slab is >300 sq ft, pour with a construction joint with waterbar.

**Curing - most important:** Pond cure for 14 days minimum. In Bengaluru you can do it, in Tirupati you must cover with wet gunny. If you don't cure, admixture does nothing.

**Then STILL add on top:** After 21 days, 15mm screed with slope 1:60 to drain + one APP membrane or 2 coats of polymer modified cementitious coating (URP type). Then your ceramic tiles on pedestals or with epoxy grout. Waterproofing costs 1-2% of construction; leakage repair runs 5-10 times higher later.

So: **Admixture is the inside protection, membrane is the outside raincoat, ceramic tile is the sun-hat.** You need all three.

With that stack:

- In Bengaluru: 60-80 years leak-free, then just replace membrane and tiles.
- In Tirupati: 50-70 years because of heat.

To hit 300 years, you still want the pitched Mangalore tile roof over the topmost flat slab, leaving a ventilated air gap. That way even if flat slab develops a crack in year 80, rain never touches it. The slab becomes a floor, not a roof.
