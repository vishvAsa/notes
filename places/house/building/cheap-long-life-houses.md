**The only inexpensive 3-story that will actually last 300 years around Bengaluru or Tirupati is a thick load-bearing stone box with lime-surkhi mortar and a pitched tile roof, not a modern cement RCC frame box.**

Modern RCC framed houses are designed for 50-70 years because the steel inside the concrete rusts when water gets in. The houses that have already lasted 300 years in India are all the same: stone or burnt brick, lime mortar, thick breathable walls, and a roof that throws water far away from the walls.

## Why normal construction fails before 100 years

1. **Cement plaster traps water.** It doesn't breathe, so damp stays inside walls and rots the steel.
2. **Flat RCC roofs leak.** In Deccan monsoon, water sits, cracks develop, steel corrodes.
3. **Thin 9-inch walls in hot climate.** They heat up fast and need constant AC and replastering.

Old havelis and Deccan houses survive because they used lime, not cement. That mortar was lime + surkhi (finely ground brick dust) + jaggery + black gram + bael fruit pulp, fermented into a breathable, self-healing binder. And stone was understood as the most resilient, long-lasting material.

For hot and dry or composite climates like Tirupati and Bengaluru, traditional wisdom is still: stone, mud blocks, and lime for insulation and longevity, and mud blocks, lime plasters and stone to insulate in hot areas.

## The 300-year formula for cheap

If you want cheap AND 300-year, you have to invert costs:

- **Foundation: rubble trench + granite footing.** 4-5 ft deep, 3 ft wide, packed Sadarahalli granite waste or rough-cut Chittoor granite. No RCC footing needed in hard Deccan soil. Build plinth 2 ft above highest flood level.
- **Walls: load-bearing, tapering.** Ground floor 18" (450mm), first floor 14" (350mm), second floor 9-10" (250mm). Every 4 ft, put a through-stone (header) across the wall width. Every floor level, a continuous lime concrete band or granite lintel band for earthquake.
- **Mortar: lime-surkhi, not cement.** 1 lime : 1.5 surkhi : 2 sand. Add fermented jaggery water. It gets stronger for 100 years and is sacrificial, so walls don't crack.
- **Plaster: lime plaster inside and outside.** 12mm first coat, 6mm second. Then lime wash every 2-3 years. Never cement plaster.
- **Slabs: filler slab or Madras terrace.** Filler slab uses Mangalore tiles / clay pots as filler, saves 30% concrete and steel. Madras terrace (teakwood or steel joists + small bricks + lime concrete) lasts 200+ years if kept dry and is cooler than RCC. For 3 stories, use filler RCC slab for first two floors with 50mm cover, and pitched Mangalore tile roof on steel or wood trusses for the top.
- **Water management is everything.** 3 ft roof overhang on all sides, French drain around building, slope ground away, no concealed plumbing inside walls. Run all pipes outside in a duct.

## How to make 3 stories functional and cheap

- **Staircase:** Stone or brick vault staircase, not steel. Steel rusts.
- **No steel where you can avoid it.** Where you need steel in slabs, use 50mm cover and add waterproofing additive, but keep slab soffits lime plastered.
- **Modular bays:** Build on 10 ft grid. Saves cutting stone and wood.
- **Reclaimed doors/windows:** Old Burma teak from demolition markets lasts another 100 years, costs half of new teak.
- **Electrics and plumbing in shafts outside.** So you can replace them in 30 years without breaking walls.

## Errors to avoid

- Cement plaster on stone/laterite. It will peel in 5 years and trap damp.
- Flat terrace without slope. Even 1% slope fails. Need min 1:80 plus tile + lime concrete weathering course.
- Black cotton soil footing without testing. Get soil test. In parts of Tirupati you hit expansive soil, you need deeper rubble or lime stabilization.
- Building too close to ground. If plinth is less than 18", capillary water will destroy lime mortar in 20 years.
