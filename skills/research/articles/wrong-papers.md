> My best rough estimate of the fraction of the scientific literature that’s wrong — flat-out wrong, you’d-be-nuts-to-rely-on-this wrong — is 80%. The problem is that the normal process of scientific self-correction takes time and happens through follow-up work that fails to reproduce experiments, finds computational errors, challenges assumptions, and so on. This is a big opportunity for AI-for-science tools. Agentic interfaces to the scientific literature should be designed so that when they look up a paper they should explicitly look for later work that challenges it. A harder but potentially far more impactful intervention is to use agents to auto-annotate papers in the pre-training corpus based on what other later sources have said about them, as well as re-weighting and other tricks. Of course AI developers shouldn’t become the arbiters of scientific truth but the fact that online information is of widely varying quality is already central to LLM pretraining recipes and agentic retrieval; my point is that there are responsible ways to apply this insight to the scientific corpus as well.
