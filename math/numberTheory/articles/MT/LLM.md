## Goldbach
Source: [TW](https://x.com/blog_supplement/status/2124174892144677324)

While Riemann has been stormed, I've not heard any rumblings of AI attempting an assault on Goldbach: Every even integer greater than $2$ is the sum of two primes. I learnt of this conjecture early in life, and even for a person like me with a rather modest IQ, it became clear that it lay in an unusual "peak". Primes are defined multiplicatively, whereas the conjecture is about an additive property of integers. Thus, if Goldbach is proven, it has the potential to bring together multiplicative structure and additive structure in arithmetic.

This makes a layman like me wonder if  Goldbach might be a Gödelian statement that is unprovable within the axioms of arithmetic. Somebody might have actually said that in print. However, there is a theorem called Chen's theorem that is believed to be the closest anyone has gotten to Goldbach: Every sufficiently large even integer can be written as the sum of a prime number and a number that is either prime or the product of at most two primes

$2n = p + P_2$ for sufficiently large $n$.
Where
$P
_2$ is either prime or a product of two primes (semiprime)

While Chen's theorem works for small even numbers, the proof itself requires colossal numbers for the "sufficiently large": $N > e^ {e^ {32.7}}$. The existence of Chen suggests that Goldbach may be provable, but that still seems to defy the AI overlord
